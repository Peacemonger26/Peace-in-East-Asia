Resisting the Arms Race: Civil Society Voices Call for Peace and Disarmament in Asia
As defense expenditures surge across Asia, regional NGOs, peace activists, and grassroots organizations are pushing back—demanding a shift from nuclear deterrence to human security and common peace frameworks.

Across Asia, the drumbeat of militarization and strategic rearmament has grown increasingly loud. Yet, beyond the headlines of expanding military budgets and strategic deterrence doctrines, a resilient network of regional civil society organizations, peace advocates, and non-governmental organizations is pushing back. From Seoul to Tokyo and beyond, grassroots groups are challenging the assumption that weapons provide security, advocating instead for human security, common peace frameworks, and complete nuclear abolition.

Reclaiming Priorities: From Defense Budgets to Human Security
In South Korea, groups like People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy (PSPD) are highlighting the hidden costs of East Asia’s escalating arms race. PSPD directly challenges spiraling defense expenditures, arguing that military spending diverts vital resources away from urgent societal needs—such as climate resilience, social welfare, and public health. Their call emphasizes a shift toward human security, urging governments to invest in human well-being and ecological sustainability rather than militarization.

Dismantling the Nuclear Deterrence Myth
Following the historic recognition of atomic bomb survivors (hibakusha), Japanese organizations such as Peace Boat and the Japan Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (JCANW) continue to lead public and parliamentary debates on disarmament. These groups argue against relying on extended nuclear deterrence—often referred to as the nuclear umbrella—and demand that regional governments commit to the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW).

"Genuine security is achieved not through nuclear umbrellas or hyper-militarized borders, but by creating institutionalized channels for mutual dialogue and regional trust."

— International Peace Bureau Hiroshima Statement
This message is reinforced globally by platforms like the International Peace Bureau (IPB). In joint statements issued from Hiroshima, peace advocates challenge the doctrine of nuclear deterrence as a dangerous myth, pointing out the catastrophic humanitarian consequences of nuclear conflict and urging regional powers to embrace genuine diplomacy.

Shifting Mindsets: Education and Common Security
Advocacy is not only about opposing weapons; it is also about building a culture of peace. Organizations such as PEACEMOMO in South Korea focus on peace education and the concept of Common Security—the principle that no country can achieve true security by making its neighbors feel insecure. By fostering regional dialogue and cross-border understanding, these educational frameworks aim to replace zero-sum military posturing with cooperative regional structures.

The Path Forward
While official policy across Asia often favors military build-ups and strategic alliances, these civil society voices offer a vital counter-narrative. By centering human needs, dismantling nuclear doctrines, and prioritizing dialogue over deterrence, regional NGOs remind us that lasting peace cannot be built through an arms race—it can only be achieved through sustained commitment to peace-building and mutual trust.
