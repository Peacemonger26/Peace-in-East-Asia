Voices Across Asia Demand Peace & Phased Disarmament Over Deterrence

As geopolitical tensions run high, civil society groups and peace advocates across Asia are speaking out against growing military spending and nuclear reliance.

Share or Reproduce this Article:

Across East Asia and the broader region, a powerful counter-narrative to escalating military budgets is taking shape. Rather than viewing national security strictly through the lens of weapon accumulation and nuclear umbrellas, civil society organizations, grassroots movements, and peace researchers are offering alternative visions centered on human security, diplomatic engagement, and phased disarmament.

From South Korea to Japan, grassroots groups are actively challenging the assumption that peace can only be guaranteed through ever-increasing military deterrence. Instead, these organizations advocate for shifting national resources toward pressing global challenges—such as climate resilience, social safety nets, and peace education—arguing that true security stems from regional cooperation rather than mutual threat.

"True security cannot be achieved by expanding arms races or relying on nuclear deterrence. True security relies on building trust, common security frameworks, and reallocating military funds toward human needs."

— Regional Civil Society Declaration

Recent initiatives highlight key focus areas for regional peace advocates:

  • Challenging the Deterrence Doctrine: Demonstrating how over-reliance on military build-ups often leads to security dilemmas rather than long-term stability.
  • Promoting Human Security: Reallocating defensive budgets to combat climate risks, economic inequality, and public health needs.
  • Advocating for Nuclear Abolition: Pushing regional governments to participate actively in international treaties like the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW).
  • Building Common Security: Fostering bilateral and multilateral dialogue channels that center joint solutions over competitive posturing.
